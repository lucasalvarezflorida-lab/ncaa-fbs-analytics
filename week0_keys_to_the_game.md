## 1. North Carolina vs TCU — Aviva Stadium, Dublin · Sat noon ET · ESPN

### What North Carolina needs to do to win
1. **Throw it, don't run it.** UNC's rush offense was #127 and TCU's run front
   #28 (Markis Deal anchors it) — that door is shut. TCU's pass defense was
   **#103** (109th per dropback). Billy Edwards Jr. (2,881 yds / 15 TD at
   Maryland in 2024; 16 attempts all of 2025) at that secondary is the entire
   path. Shipp (60-671-6) and Kekahuna underneath, the three transfer TEs in
   the middle.
2. **Win the hidden yards.** Special teams #31 vs TCU's #84. A quarterback
   with a 16-attempt 2025 needs short fields; Rece Verhoff is kicking on a
   contested eligibility ruling — know that going in.
3. **Bend-don't-break without Steve Belichick.** The 2025 defense (59th SP+)
   was the bright spot; its coordinator is on indefinite medical leave and no
   interim has been named. Abou Jaoude (10.5 sacks) has to make Jaden Craig's
   first FBS start uncomfortable without the scheme getting complicated.
4. **Keep the circus outside.** Lombardi on leave, the DC away, a media
   blackout all camp. Petrino's first 15 scripted plays and a clean
   first quarter matter more for this team than most.

### What TCU needs to do to win
1. **Let Craig be efficient, not heroic.** 48 TD / 10 INT over two Ivy
   seasons, 61.6% and 2,829 yds in 2025 — the #29 pass offense infrastructure
   (Dwyer 54-730-7, Ed Small, Jeremy Scott) is there; the job is distribution,
   not a showcase. UNC's pass defense was a respectable #49.
2. **Make UNC one-dimensional, then rush.** The #28 run front turns UNC
   (#127) into a dropback team on schedule; six of seven linemen return, and
   Oyewale / Ofili / Hayward are the pressure bet against a QB coming off a
   lost year.
3. **Fix the dropback defense with a new safety.** TCU was 19th vs the run and
   109th per dropback in 2025; projected starter Jacob Fields was dismissed
   Aug 18 and Jamel Johnson (5 INT) moves up. UNC's only path runs straight
   through this group.
4. **Cash the special-teams edge the other way.** Dwyer averaged 11.0 per punt
   return (no TDs) — the unit ranked #84, so the returner is the weapon, not
   the unit.

**The machine's read:** coin-flip-plus for TCU (54%); the market says ~73%.
The market is pricing UNC's chaos; the machine can't see it. Research, not a
play.

## 2. NC State at Virginia — Scott Stadium · Sat 3:30 ET · ESPN

### What NC State needs to do to win
1. **Bailey has to beat the #26 pass defense with a rebuilt room.** CJ Bailey
   (3,105 yds, 25-9, 68.8%) is the best returning QB in the ACC, but his top
   four catchers are gone and Teddy Hoffmann is out all year (PED). JoJo
   Trader (Miami, 13-178-1) and Victor Snow have to be real on day one.
2. **Establish Duke Scott.** 595 yds at 5.6 a carry against a UVA run
   defense that was #42 — the run keeps Bailey out of obvious passing downs,
   which is where the #26 pass D lives.
3. **Stop losing games on special teams.** NCSU #101 vs UVA #56; ST cost State
   two games in 2025. Scott's kick returns (234 yds) are the one plus.
4. **Make Pribula the story.** 11-9 TD-INT at Missouri. New edges Harvey Dyson
   (8 sacks at Tulane) and Katron Evans (if eligible — Doeren says situations
   are pending) against "the most experienced O-line in the country."

### What Virginia needs to do to win
1. **Pribula ball security.** 9 INT in 270 attempts at Missouri; the 2025
   miracle ran on turnover luck and the residual (+11.7, 11th-highest in FBS)
   says the rating is carrying that forward. Even 67.4% completions and six
   rushing TDs don't survive three giveaways.
2. **Ride the O-line and the #33 rush offense.** Josey and Boley up front;
   Peyton Lewis (7 TD at Tennessee) and Middlebrook against an NCSU run
   defense that was #81. Beebe (arrested Aug 17) was RB3 and the primary KR —
   depth, not the headline.
3. **Replace the pass rush, not just the pass rushers.** Rickert (6.5) and
   Melton (5) graduated; Camac (3.5 sacks) and four portal edges — Ezekiel
   Larry (10.5 at Yale) wasn't cleared for full contact as of Aug 12. Bailey
   with time is the loss condition.
4. **Let the #26 pass defense win a rebuilt-receiver matchup.** Jam Jackson
   and Platt against a State WR room with one proven player.

**The machine's read:** UVA 65%, but the stated edge is gone — the books
converged from −3 / −6 to −5.5, and the machine's −6.7 is the stalest number
on the card. No play.

## 3. Jacksonville State at North Dakota State — Fargodome · Sat 5:30 ET · CBSSN

### What Jacksonville State needs to do to win
1. **Run into a front with no FBS tape.** #23 rush offense, back-to-back
   ~3,500-yard seasons; Caden Creel ran for 1,075 (5.9 a carry, 7 TD). RB1
   Tre Cook left for WVU, so it's Lando / Paul / Savage by committee — the
   identity has to survive the personnel change.
2. **Stay ahead of the chains.** The pass game was #107 (Creel 61.6%, 1,514
   yds, 9-4). If the Bison make this a 30-attempt night for Creel, JSU is
   playing their game.
3. **Don't leak hidden yardage indoors.** Special teams #123. In the
   Fargodome, with a crowd that's a force, short fields compound fast.
4. **Make Nathan Hayes beat you.** First career start in five years on
   campus. Disguise coverages, force third-and-long, and test the "strongest
   arm since Wentz" line under real pressure. Khurtiss Perry (4.5 TFL) and
   Jacob Cruz (1 INT) need one takeaway.

### What North Dakota State needs to do to win
1. **Protect Hayes early.** His 2025 sample is 25-of-44 for 381 in relief;
   the first quarter should be scripted, short, and on schedule. A clean
   first drive matters more than a big one.
2. **Lean on the run and the line.** DJ Scott (502 yds, 5.3 a carry, 6 TD)
   behind a dynasty O-line against JSU's transfer-restocked front (Toombs,
   Rumph + returning Perry). This is where NDSU's polish shows or doesn't.
3. **Tackle Creel in space.** The rebuilt CB room is the one unit with no
   returning proof; Creel's legs are the Bison defense's soft spot.
4. **Take the free points.** JSU's #123 special teams will hand over field
   position; the Bison have to convert it.

**The machine's read:** NDSU 57% — but NDSU has zero FBS ratings history, so
that number deserves a range, not a point. Line moved from −10 to −7 since
July (CLV +3 on the JSU side). Note: NDSU is eligible for the MWC title game,
bowls and the CFP this year — the transition ban was repealed June 24.

## 4. Hawai'i at Stanford — Stanford Stadium · Sat 7:00 ET · ACC Network

### What Hawai'i needs to do to win
1. **Volume through the air at a #115 pass defense.** Micah Alejado (66.3%,
   3,106 yds, 24-9; MWC preseason POY) in the run-and-shoot against a
   secondary that was 115th. That is the whole game plan — and it works only
   if the receivers show up.
2. **Find the targets if Ashlock can't go.** WR1 Pofele Ashlock (76-827-8)
   sat all camp with a knee and had no return timeline as of Aug 11; Jackson
   Harris (963 yds) is gone. Audric Harris (Washington), Griffiths, Brown
   have to be the room.
3. **Keep Alejado upright against the #15 run front.** Hawai'i's line lags
   (Lipe Moala from Oregon helps); Stanford's front seven — Matt Rose, 106
   tackles — is the one good unit they have.
4. **Three stops, not ten — and don't lose the kicking game.** The 3-3-5 is
   undersized, but Stanford's offense was #128 on the ground and the QB is
   nine months off an ACL. Special teams were #8 last year, but that was
   Matsuzawa (27/29) — a JC walk-on, Sean Olvera-Harle, kicks now.

### What Stanford needs to do to win
1. **Warren efficient, not expansive.** Davis Warren (Michigan) — first game
   back from a December 2024 ACL, career 5.4 yards per dropback, 7 TD / 10
   INT. Nico Brown (71-1,085-11 at Yale) is the outlet; the ask is no
   turnovers against a defense that forces three stops, not ten.
2. **Make the run game exist.** #128 last year at 3.9 per non-sack carry;
   Micah Ford (643 yds, 4.4) behind Simione Pale against a Hawai'i run
   defense that was #70. Any balance changes this game.
3. **Tackle in space against four wide.** Kris Richard's first game: Rose
   and Brandon Nicholson (2 INT, 6 PD) have to limit explosives from a
   system that averaged 394 yards and 29 points.
4. **Hold serve on special teams.** Stanford was #125 (Kenney 16/21). Against
   a unit that was #8 — even with the new kicker — this is where a two-point
   game goes.

**The machine's read:** it rates Hawai'i the better team (Stanford −1.5 only
because of home field); the market has gone the other way, from −3 at the
open to −5.5. Lean Hawai'i +5.5, a four-point gap, not a conviction.

## 5. Memphis at UNLV — Allegiant Stadium · Sat 10:00 ET · FOX

### What Memphis needs to do to win
1. **Don't ask the quarterback to win it.** Huff has picked between Marcus
   Stokes (3,297 yds / 30 TD at D-II West Florida) and Air Noland (three
   passes at South Carolina in 2025) and won't say who until kickoff. Either
   way it's a zero-FBS-snap tier; the plan has to be run-first.
2. **Run at the #130 run defense.** UNLV's run D was the worst in the
   league-and-a-half; Dallan Hayden (4.7 a carry at Colorado) and Manny Covey
   (7.2 at Cincinnati) behind a line with one returnee (Parker Mitchell) plus
   four G6 starters. Memphis's #5 rush-offense rank was Silverfield's roster;
   this is the first look at Huff's.
3. **Tapp has to get home.** J'Mond Tapp (69 tkl, 12 TFL, 7.5 sacks, 10
   hurries at USM) is the most proven defender on a roster that's 53
   transfers new. Jackson Arnold with time against a #118 pass defense is the
   loss condition.
4. **Survive the hidden-yardage swing.** Memphis's #16 special-teams rank was
   Sutton Smith (99-yd KR TD, 8.3 PR avg) — he's gone. UNLV's unit was #65.

### What UNLV needs to do to win
1. **Feed Jai'Den Thomas.** 1,034 yds at 7.0 a carry, 12 TD, plus 38 catches —
   the #9 rush offense against a Memphis run defense that was #20 last year
   but is now built from Southern Miss transfers.
2. **No turnovers from the QB, whoever it is.** Arnold (63.3%, 1,309 yds, 6-2
   in eight Auburn starts, +311 rushing) or Orji — Mullen says both may play.
   Against a #118 pass defense the bar is "don't give it away," not "light it
   up."
3. **Make the new receivers real.** Every 2025 target is gone (Bradley 931,
   Omeire, Reynolds); Reddicks (Oregon State), Stellato (Kentucky), Walker
   (MTSU) arrive via the portal. Someone has to be Arnold's first read.
4. **The #130 run defense cannot be #130 against Huff-ball.** Dee Crayton
   (Clemson, 5 tackles as a reserve) and Herb Gray (Tennessee) are the
   patches; Fuller, Welch and Spellman are playing on an eligibility ruling
   the NCAA is appealing. This is the unit the whole game turns on.

**The machine's read:** UNLV 64%, market 67% — the two agree now. The edge
that existed at −3 is gone, and the line moved our way (CLV +2.5). A watch and
the first `film_study` run, not a play.
