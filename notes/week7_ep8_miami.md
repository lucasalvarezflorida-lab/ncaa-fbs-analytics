  - The history
    - Miami leads 37–33 and has won two straight: 28–22 last year in Tallahassee, 36–14 in 2024; Florida State won 27–20 in 2023 and 45–3 in 2022. Miami's last decisive home win in the series was 45–10 in 2020.
    - The peak was 1985 to 1994: seven straight top-10 meetings, four of them top-five, Wide Right I and II, the "Game of the Century," two Heisman winners, four national titles between them (three Miami, one Florida State). Wide Right I in 1991 decided a national championship; Wide Right II in 1992 did it again.
    - Miami won six straight from 2000 to 2004; Florida State took six straight from 2010 to 2015. Each program has had its run; they have rarely been good at the same time.
    - Both have been in the ACC since 2004, and under the conference's scheduling model the game is a protected annual rivalry — it never leaves the calendar.
  - Why it fell off
    - The rivalry's value was two top-five teams, and since 2004 the pairs have mostly been one good team and one rebuilding. The low point was 2021: a combined record of 8–10.
    - Miami's two decades of coaching churn (Coker, Shannon, Golden, Richt, Diaz) and a stadium that is not on campus drained one side; Florida State's collapse after 2014 drained the other.
    - The conference setting changed the stakes: in the Big East-vs-independent era the game was a national-title eliminator; in the ACC it became a division game, and now one protected game among five with no built-in path to the title game.
    - This year is the closest it has come since 2004: one top-five team, not two.
  - Why Miami wins

    - Daniels is a runner (10 rushing touchdowns, a school record five against Virginia) throwing 8.6 a throw with eight touchdowns against seven interceptions. Miami's defense is built for a quarterback who holds the ball: Hetherman's sim-pressure front, Bain, a 23.5% stuff rate, 9.8 points allowed a game, first in the country.
